# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Pablo Caro writes technical notes for his future self — Linux recipes, Python patterns, AI experiments, tool comparisons. The notes are publicly shared and attract other developers through search and social media. Readers arrive with a specific question or curiosity, scan for answers, and leave. They value clarity and directness over personality or presentation.

Context: reading on desktop during work breaks, on mobile while commuting, or late at night looking up a forgotten command.

## Product Purpose

A personal knowledge garden publicly shared under the name _Anotaciones_ (Annotations). The blog is Pablo's external memory — things he doesn't want occupying mental space. It is also a professional presence: a portfolio of thinking, a demonstration of competence, and a way to contribute back to the developer community.

Success looks like: Pablo finds his old notes useful; strangers email to say a post saved them time; the site feels calm, readable, and unmistakably his.

## Brand Personality

**Practical, clean, personal.** The blog is unfussy and warm. It never tries to be clever or attention-grabbing. Content comes first. The design serves the reading experience — nothing decorative, nothing that slows down scanning. It feels like a well-organized notebook, not a brand.

The writing style reinforces this: direct, pragmatic, code-first, no long intros. "Notes for myself" energy that happens to help others.

## Anti-references

- **No friky/nerdy aesthetic** — not a terminal simulator, no monospace everywhere, no hacker-culture visual tropes. This is a human writing for humans who happen to code.
- **No generic SaaS-template look** — not the cream/blue gradient corporate blog, not the cookie-cutter developer portfolio. Avoids the "another static site" feel.
- **No content-obscuring flair** — no heavy animations, no aggressive branding, no design that competes with the words for attention.

## Design Principles

1. **Content is the only product.** Every design decision serves readability and scanability. If a visual element doesn't help someone find or consume technical content faster, it shouldn't exist.

2. **Cleanliness is kindness.** For readers arriving with a specific question, clutter is friction. Generous whitespace, clear hierarchy, obvious navigation. No surprises.

3. **Personal warmth over professional polish.** The site should feel like Pablo's desk — organized but lived-in. Warm neutrals and a single restrained accent. Not sterile.

4. **Bilingual by design.** Spanish is the home language, English is the guest language — and both should feel equally at home. Language switching must be obvious and effortless.

5. **Code is content, content is code.** Posts are code-heavy. Syntax highlighting, inline code, and code blocks must be as carefully considered as body text — not an afterthought.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Semantic HTML (already in place via Pelican/Flex)
- Dark/light mode support (already in place)
- Readable contrast ratios as a baseline
- Reduced motion respected for users who prefer it
- Bilingual site: both languages treated as first-class
